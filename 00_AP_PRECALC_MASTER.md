## Mental Model — What This Exam Actually Tests

AP Precalc tests four moves repeatedly:

1. **Translation between representations.** Graph ↔ table ↔ equation ↔ verbal description. If you can fluidly move between them, you can solve almost any FRQ.

2. **Rate of change reasoning.** Average rate of change, concavity, "increasing at increasing/decreasing rate," local extrema, end behavior. The exam returns to this every unit.

3. **Function manipulation.** Composition, inverse, transformation, equivalent algebraic forms. Speed here is decisive.

4. **Modeling and selection.** Given data or context, which function class fits? Why? What does the model predict? When does it fail?

If you internalize these four, you stop seeing "polynomial problems" or "trig problems" — you see one of the four moves played in different costumes.

## The Speed Standard

**MCQ Section I Part A:** 80 min ÷ 28 = ~2.85 min per question. Some you finish in 30 seconds, banking time for the harder ones.

**MCQ Section I Part B:** 40 min ÷ 12 = ~3.33 min per question. Calculator-allowed → setup is the work, computation is fast.

**FRQ:** 15 min per question. Each question has 3 parts (A, B, C), each part has 2–3 sub-parts. ~2 min per sub-part.

If you're stuck on a question for more than ~4 minutes (MCQ) or more than ~8 minutes (FRQ), flag and move. Come back if time.

## Calculator Discipline

You are required to have a graphing calculator for Sections IB and IIA. Use it for:
- Solving equations numerically (intersect, zero, solver)
- Evaluating expressions to many decimal places
- Producing a residual plot or regression
- Graphing to identify features (max, min, intersection, asymptote)
- Storing intermediate values to avoid rounding error

**Rule:** When the question allows the calculator, do not waste effort on algebra you can avoid. When it disallows it, exact answers in radicals/logs/π are expected — write them that way.

**Rule:** Decimal answers must be accurate to **three decimal places** unless otherwise specified. Round at the end, never mid-computation.

**Rule:** Calculator must be in **radians** for trig. Verify on the first trig question of each section.

## FRQ Scoring — How To Earn Points

Each FRQ is worth 6 points. Points are distributed across sub-parts. Two failure modes lose points:

1. **Stating an answer without supporting work** — the rubric says "answers without supporting work may not receive credit." Always show the equation you set up, the substitution, the simplification. Even if obvious.

2. **Arithmetic errors that compound** — they grade communication of method. If your method is right but your number is wrong, partial credit. If your method is wrong but your number happens to be right, often zero.

**Good habits in the FRQ booklet:**
- Label each sub-part (A.i., A.ii., B.i., etc.) clearly.
- Define variables when you use them.
- For limits, write the limit notation: $\lim_{x \to -3} g(x) = 7/6$, not just "7/6 at x = -3."
- For modeling parts, state what your variables represent.
- For interpretation parts, write a complete sentence including units.

## Key Definitions Worth Carrying In Head

**Average rate of change** of $f$ on $[a, b]$:
$$\text{ARC} = \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}$$

**Increasing at increasing rate** = increasing AND concave up.
**Increasing at decreasing rate** = increasing AND concave down.
**Decreasing at increasing rate** = decreasing AND concave down (slope going from very negative toward 0, so slope is increasing).
**Decreasing at decreasing rate** = decreasing AND concave up (slope going from 0 toward very negative, so slope is decreasing). 

Drill these. They appear on every exam, and the wording trips people up.

**Concavity diagnostic:** Concave up = curve "smiles," lies above its tangent lines. Concave down = curve "frowns," lies below its tangent lines.

## What Wins This Exam

Three things, in order:

1. **Speed on standard moves.** If you have to think about how to factor a quadratic, you're behind. The first 20 MCQ in Section IA should feel automatic.

2. **Recognition of question type.** "This is a sinusoidal modeling question — period from peak-to-peak, amplitude from (max − min)/2, midline from (max + min)/2, phase shift to align." Pattern recognition saves 90 seconds per question.

3. **Discipline on the FRQ booklet.** Show work. Label parts. Use limit notation. Write interpretive sentences with units. Easy points lost by careless presentation are the difference between a 4 and a 5.
